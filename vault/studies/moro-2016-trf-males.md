## Summary

Thirty-four healthy, resistance-trained males (≥5 years continuous resistance-training
experience, steroid-free, mean age 29.2 ± 3.8 y) were randomized to 8 weeks of 16:8
time-restricted feeding (TRF — all calories consumed in an 8-hour window, three meals at
1 p.m., 4 p.m., and 8 p.m.) or a normal-diet (ND) control with matched total calories and
macronutrients, while continuing an unchanged resistance-training program. The trial
measured body composition, resting energy expenditure, strength, and a panel of hormonal,
inflammatory, and lipid biomarkers.

## Key findings

- **Fat mass:** TRF reduced fat mass significantly more than ND (−16.4% vs. −2.8%,
  p = 0.0448).
- **Fat-free (lean) mass:** maintained in both groups, with no significant
  between-group difference (TRF +0.86% vs. ND +0.64%) — TRF did not cost lean mass.
- **Strength:** maintained across the intervention in both groups per the study's
  reported strength outcomes.
- **Testosterone:** declined significantly in the TRF group relative to ND
  (p = 0.0476).
- **IGF-1:** declined significantly in the TRF group (−28.0 ng/mL) vs. essentially no
  change in ND (+3.23 ng/mL), p = 0.0397.
- **Adipokines:** adiponectin increased and leptin decreased in the TRF group (reported
  in the abstract as secondary hormonal findings).
- **Resting energy expenditure:** unchanged between groups; a significant decrease in
  respiratory ratio was observed with TRF.

## Limitations

- Small sample (N = 34, single site) — likely underpowered for some secondary hormonal
  and inflammatory endpoints.
- Population is narrow: healthy, steroid-free, resistance-trained young men only — does
  not generalize to untrained, older, unhealthy, or female populations.
- Dietary intake was assessed by interview/recall, a method the authors note has
  "known weaknesses"; possible unmeasured differences in actual intake between groups.
- Meal timing relative to training sessions was not standardized across all
  participants and could itself affect the biomarker responses observed.
