> not sex-stratified — no analysis isolates the effect for women vs. the male minority.

## Summary

Fifty-eight adults with obesity (BMI 30.0–49.9 kg/m², age 18–65, ~90% female, insulin
resistant by HOMA-IR ≥ 2.7 at screening, normotensive, normocholesterolemic) were
randomized to an 8-week trial (after a 2-week baseline) of 4-hour time-restricted feeding
(eating only 3–7 p.m.), 6-hour time-restricted feeding (eating only 1–7 p.m.), or a
no-meal-timing-restriction control. 49 completed the trial (16 in 4h-TRF, 19 in 6h-TRF,
14 in control). This was the first RCT to directly compare two eating-window lengths for
weight and cardiometabolic outcomes (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03867773).

## Key findings

- **Body weight:** both TRF arms lost ~3.2% ± 0.4% of body weight vs. +0.1% ± 0.4% in
  control over 8 weeks (p < 0.001 for each TRF arm vs. control); 4h and 6h did not differ
  significantly from each other (p > 0.05). Neither arm reached the study's own 5%
  threshold for "clinically significant" weight loss at 8 weeks.
- **Insulin resistance (HOMA-IR):** improved in both TRF arms vs. control — 4h-TRF
  ~29% reduction (p = 0.03 vs. control), 6h-TRF ~12% reduction (p = 0.04 vs. control);
  control HOMA-IR rose over the same period.
- **Fasting insulin:** decreased in both TRF arms (4h: −2.3 ± 1.5 µIU/mL, p = 0.02;
  6h: −1.9 ± 1.1 µIU/mL, p = 0.04) vs. an increase in control.
- **Energy intake:** both TRF arms spontaneously reduced intake by ~550 kcal/day
  (~30%) without explicit calorie counting (p = 0.008 vs. control) — a likely mechanism
  for the weight loss.
- **Oxidative stress:** reduced in both TRF arms relative to control (single marker,
  8-isoprostane).

## Limitations

- Small sample, and the authors state they were underpowered for some secondary
  outcomes.
- No comparison of *when* the window fell in the day (early vs. late timing) — only
  window *length* (4h vs. 6h) was varied; both windows ended at 7 p.m.
- Single oxidative-stress marker measured.
- Not a crossover design.
- No sex-stratified analysis and no discussion in the paper of sex-specific
  generalizability, despite the cohort being ~90% female — see gap flag above.
