## Overview

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a common endocrine condition in women of
reproductive age, characterized by some combination of irregular or absent ovulation,
hyperandrogenism (clinical or biochemical), and polycystic ovarian morphology. Insulin
resistance is present in a large share of cases and is a frequent target of dietary
intervention. No study currently in this vault enrolled or reported on a PCOS
population specifically.

## Why fasting protocols for PCOS are an open question

PCOS commonly co-occurs with insulin resistance, which makes TRF's glucose/insulin
effects ([[claims/trf-glucose-women-evidence]]) directly relevant *in principle* — but
that claim is graded C precisely because its supporting trial
([[studies/cienfuegos-2020-4h-6h-trf]]) was not sex-stratified and did not report a
PCOS subgroup. PCOS also involves androgen physiology, and the only trial in this vault
that measured sex hormones, [[studies/moro-2016-trf-males]], found TRF *lowered*
testosterone — but that study enrolled only men, and a testosterone-lowering effect in a
healthy man is not obviously the same clinical direction of interest as in a
hyperandrogenic woman with PCOS; the finding cannot be assumed to transfer. Until a trial
enrolls and reports on a PCOS population directly, any TRF-for-PCOS product claim is
extrapolation, not evidence — see [[interventions/16-8-fasting]]'s Safety flags and
Open questions.
