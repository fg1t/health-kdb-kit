## What it is

16:8 time-restricted eating (TRF/TRE) confines all daily caloric intake to an 8-hour
window, fasting (water/non-caloric beverages only) for the remaining 16 hours. It
specifies nothing about *what* or *how much* to eat inside the window — only *when*. The
two trials in this vault use a fixed daily window (e.g. 1 p.m.–9 p.m. in
[[studies/moro-2016-trf-males]]; 3–7 p.m. or 1–7 p.m. in
[[studies/cienfuegos-2020-4h-6h-trf]]) rather than a window that shifts day to day.

## Evidence by population

### Resistance-trained men

[[studies/moro-2016-trf-males]] (narrative tier, no claim extracted yet): an 8-week RCT
in 34 resistance-trained males found TRF reduced fat mass significantly more than a
normal diet (−16.4% vs. −2.8%, p = 0.0448) while maintaining lean mass, but also lowered
testosterone and IGF-1 significantly relative to the normal-diet group. Male-only
cohort — ⚠️ see the gap note below.

### Adults with obesity (majority-female trial)

[[claims/trf-weight-loss-adults]] (grade B): TRF (4h or 6h window, evidence from
[[studies/cienfuegos-2020-4h-6h-trf]]) produced ~3% body-weight loss vs. control
over 8 weeks in adults with obesity, with no difference between window lengths.

[[claims/trf-glucose-women-evidence]] (grade C, population-adjusted): the same trial
found improved insulin resistance (HOMA-IR), in a completer cohort that was ~90% female
— but the result was never sex-stratified.

### Women specifically

⚠️ **Women-specific RCT evidence thin.** No trial in this vault enrolled a comparably
sized male cohort *and* ran a sex-stratified analysis that would let us state a TRF
effect size *for women* with confidence. The [[studies/cienfuegos-2020-4h-6h-trf]]
cohort was mostly female by enrollment happenstance (obesity-clinic recruitment skewed
female), not by study design, and its results were pooled rather than split by sex.
[[studies/moro-2016-trf-males]] excluded women outright. Treat any TRF effect claimed
"for women" in product copy as extrapolated from a majority-female *pooled* sample, not
a demonstrated sex-specific effect.

## Open questions

- Does TRF's hormonal effect in trained men (testosterone/IGF-1 ↓, per
  [[studies/moro-2016-trf-males]]) occur — or matter — in women, who were entirely
  excluded from that study?
- Would a sex-stratified re-analysis of a Cienfuegos-style trial change the
  glucose/insulin effect-size estimate specifically for women?
- No trial in this vault isolates PCOS or other female-specific endocrine conditions —
  see [[conditions/pcos]].

## Safety flags

- **Pregnancy / trying to conceive:** neither trial in this vault studied pregnant or
  conceiving participants; TRF's metabolic and caloric effects in pregnancy are unknown
  from this evidence base — do not extend these findings to that population.
- **Eating-disorder history:** neither trial screened for, or reports on, eating-disorder
  history; a structured fasting window can reinforce restrictive eating patterns —
  exclude or flag for clinical review.
- **Diabetes / glucose-lowering medication:** [[studies/cienfuegos-2020-4h-6h-trf]]
  enrolled an insulin-resistant but not diabetic cohort; combining TRF with insulin or
  sulfonylureas needs medical supervision (hypoglycemia risk with a compressed eating
  window) — this evidence base does not cover that combination.
- **Underweight / low body weight:** both trials enrolled either resistance-trained men
  at healthy body composition or adults with obesity (BMI 30–49.9) — no evidence here
  supports TRF for underweight individuals.
- **Adolescents:** both trials enrolled adults only (Moro: mean age ~29 y; Cienfuegos:
  18–65 y) — no evidence here for adolescent use.
